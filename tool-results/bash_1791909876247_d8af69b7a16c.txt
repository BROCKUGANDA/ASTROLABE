## 1. Summary

An employee joining Emirates or moving between roles, teams, or locations passes through dozens of steps owned by HR, IT, learning, facilities, immigration, payroll, and two managers. The steps exist; the map does not. Employees do not know what is next, who owns it, or whether the silence they are experiencing is normal. Problems surface as complaints and escalations, days after the signal was available.

ASTROLABE is an employee-side AI companion that holds the journey map, knows exactly where the employee stands on it, answers policy questions with a citation to the governing document, starts and completes routine requests as complete correctly-routed packages, detects friction from events, request telemetry, and opt-in pulse feedback, and escalates to a named human owner with a ready-made summary when the rules say a human is needed.

The thesis: journey intelligence is a **state and timing problem**, not a chat problem. Chat is the surface. A deterministic engine over an append-only event log is the product. That separation is what makes the companion auditable, defensible under HR scrutiny, and portable to any other government entity with a different journey map.

## 2. Problem

- A journey spans many owners and systems; nobody holds the end-to-end view, least of all the employee.
- The employee's default action when stuck is to wait, then complain. Both are expensive and late.
- Warning signals (a step past its target, a request bouncing between queues, a visa dependency frozen, a declining pulse score) exist in separate systems and never get correlated.
- Service owners receive incomplete requests, so the first action on most tickets is to ask the employee for something they were never told to bring.
- HR business partners discover stuck people through escalation channels, with no structured handoff and no history.
- An HR assistant that reads free-text comments and documents is an attack surface and a privacy surface at the same time. Both must be product features, not disclaimers.

## 4. Scope

**In scope for v1 (prototype build)**

- Two journeys only: **onboarding** and **internal mobility**, driven by `journey_maps.md` as declarative configuration, not code.
- Deterministic journey engine: stage inference, next-best-step, SLA clocks with working-calendar adjustment, dependency graph, 42-signal friction pack, banded verdicts.
- Grounded policy answering over `policies/` with mandatory citation, abstain-and-handoff behaviour, and English + Arabic answers.
- Request intake and handoff: typed intake schemas per request type, completeness validation, owner resolution, idempotent submission, status tracking, simulated downstream agents (HR, IT, learning, facilities) behind real interfaces.
- Opt-in pulse capture with granular consent, revocation, and a complete "what we hold about you" self-view and export.
- Escalation packets to a named human owner with evidence, timeline, and requested decision.
- HRBP console (ranked friction queue), manager view (aggregate, k-anonymous), stall analytics heatmap by stage and owner.
- Judge mode: scenario switcher, as-of clock, engine trace panel, degraded-mode simulator.
- Backtest harness with replay-at-time, labelled scenarios, and acceptance gates in CI.

**Out of scope for v1 (named as roadmap, not built)**

- Full employee lifecycle (performance, leave, offboarding, succession).
- Live Emirates system integrations; all adapters are synthetic behind production-shaped interfaces.
- Any use of ASTROLABE output in performance, disciplinary, promotion, or visa decisions. Structurally blocked, not merely discouraged.
- Per-employee sentiment exposure to managers, autonomous actions on an employee's behalf, and irreversible writes.
- Native mobile apps, enterprise SSO/SCIM provisioning, Arabic voice input.

## 5. Product principles

1. **The engine decides, the model explains.** Bands, routes, and escalations come from rules over the event log. The model never emits a score.
2. **Every policy claim cites its source.** Policy answers render as quote + document + section + effective date, or they do not render.
3. **Unsure is a first-class answer.** Below the grounding threshold the companion says what it does not know and hands off to the named owner.
4. **For the employee, never on the employee.** No manager or HRBP surface exposes transcripts, sentiment text, or per-person pulse scores.
5. **Consent is a gate, not a banner.** Signal families are suppressed in computation when consent is absent, and the suppression is visible in the explanation.
6. **Propose, approve, act.** Every state change is typed, idempotent, audited, and confirmed by a human who owns it.
7. **Degrade loudly.** When retrieval or the model fails, say which layer is missing and still answer deterministically.
8. **Replayable or it did not happen.** Derived state is a pure function of the event log plus the journey map version.
9. **Bilingual by construction.** Arabic is a first-class locale with RTL layout and parallel policy citations, not a translation afterthought.
10. **Readable from the back of the room.** Projector legibility and keyboard-only operation are acceptance criteria.

## 8. Core journeys

**E1 — "Where am I?" (primary, demoed).** Open companion → Today screen: stage chip (Onboarding · day 5), progress rail, next best step with owner and target date, late items in a Friction band card, one-tap actions.

**E2 — Ask a policy question.** "How many days of relocation allowance do I get?" → staged progress (checking your journey → reading policy → drafting) → answer with inline citation chips → "View source" opens the exact policy section → follow-up suggestions → optional "start the request" button.

**E3 — Start a routine request.** "My laptop hasn't arrived" → engine recognises the IT kit step is 3 days past target → prefilled intake (employee, location, stage, kit type, existing ticket reference) → completeness check → confirm preview → routed to the owning queue with idempotency key → status card on Requests.

**E4 — Stuck dependency, early.** Visa step past target → engine raises DP-02 (weight 7, hard trip) → employee sees honest status, what it blocks, and what they can do now → companion asks consent to share summary with HRBP → escalation packet to named HRBP.

**E5 — Opt-in pulse.** At a stage gate: "Two questions, only if you want to." Response stored with consent record; low confidence adds PS-01 to the employee's own friction view and (only if sharing consented) an aggregate signal to the HRBP queue, never the free text.

**E6 — My data.** Me → Data held about me: every record, source, purpose, retention, who viewed it, with export and revoke.

**H1 — HRBP queue.** Ranked by band and lead time: employee, stage, top three signals with evidence, suggested owner, claim button → claiming writes an audit event and notifies the employee that a human is on it.

**H2 — Handoff.** Open packet → edit summary → assign to service owner or manager → due date → employee sees "HRBP Fatima is handling this".

**M1v — Manager view.** My new joiners: steps **I** own, aggregate stage completion, no sentiment, no chat, no scores per person below the k-threshold.

**S1 — Service owner intake.** Ticket arrives with complete payload, journey context, policy reference, and a one-click "needs more info" that returns a structured question to the employee rather than a free-text email.

## 10. Deterministic journey engine

**Stage inference.** For employee `e` at time `T`: filter events to `occurred_at <= T`, map to steps via `journey_map`, compute per-step status (`not_started | in_progress | waiting_employee | waiting_owner | blocked | done | skipped`), then stage = the latest stage whose entry condition is satisfied and whose exit condition is not. Ties resolve to the earlier stage (conservative). Events for unknown steps raise DQ-05 and are listed as "unmapped activity", never dropped.

**Next best step.** Candidates = steps in the current and next stage with status not done, dependencies satisfied, filtered by actor. Rank by: hard breach > blocks the most downstream steps > nearest due date > employee-actionable > shortest estimated effort. Return top 1 primary + up to 3 secondary, each with owner, target date, policy reference, and the action that starts it.

**SLA clock.** `due_at = start + target_hours` adjusted by the location working calendar (weekend pattern, public holidays, employee leave if present). Breach levels: `none < due_soon (<20% remaining) < breached (>100%) < hard (>200%)`. Clock pauses while `waiting_employee` and that pause is shown to the employee, so the system never blames the employee silently or credits itself falsely.

**Dependency graph.** Steps form a DAG from `depends_on`. Cycles are a config error that fails CI. Blocked steps report the root blocker, the chain length, and the count of downstream steps at risk — that count drives signal weight.

**State machine (request).** `draft → validated → submitted → acknowledged → in_progress → waiting_employee ⇄ in_progress → resolved → closed`, with `returned` and `reassigned` as counted side-transitions and `cancelled` terminal. Illegal transitions are rejected with `E_CONFLICT` and logged. Every transition emits an event; status is never written directly.

## 12. Routing, intake completeness, and owner resolution

**Owner resolution order:** step owner in `journey_map` → queue for `(request_type, location)` → functional fallback (HR / IT / learning / facilities) → HRBP of record → `E_NO_OWNER` (RQ-05) with an explicit "I could not find an owner, here is who I told instead". The companion never invents a recipient.

| Request type | Required intake fields | Owner | SLA | Auto-attached context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IT kit / device | kit type, delivery location, stage, start date, prior ticket | IT provisioning | 24h | Step state, prior kit events |
| System access | system, role profile, manager approver, effective date | IT access | 48h | Role change event, transfer approval |
| Visa / work authorisation | passport-doc checklist state, dependants flag, current step | Immigration (HR) | 72h | Blocked-downstream list, policy section |
| Relocation / housing | family size, arrival date, temporary-housing flag, allowance band | Mobility (HR) | 72h | Relocation policy citation, deadlines |
| Payroll / allowance | change type, effective date, bank-confirmed flag, cycle cutoff | Payroll | 48h | MB-04 state, cutoff date |
| Training booking | course code, prerequisite status, preferred window | Learning | 48h | Prerequisite access state |
| Facilities / badge / desk | site, floor, access group, mobility flag | Facilities | 24h | Location change event |
| Manager handover | from-manager, to-manager, handover items, effective date | Both managers | 5d | Transfer approval |

Intake rules: no submission until the type schema validates; every prefilled field is labelled as prefilled and editable; a preview shows **exactly** what will be sent and to whom; submission is idempotent on `(employee_id, type, payload_hash, 24h window)` and returns the existing ticket on replay (RQ-04 dedup).

## 13. Policy answering

**Pipeline.** Normalise question → classify intent (policy | journey | action | smalltalk | out-of-scope) → retrieve: BM25 + vector hybrid over `policy_chunk`, locale-aware, reranked, top-k 8 → ground: the composer may use only retrieved chunk text + the employee's own journey facts → validate: every sentence containing a normative claim (amount, duration, eligibility, requirement) must map to a cited chunk ≥ similarity threshold → render answer with citation chips → log question, chunks, grounding score.

**Contract.** Citation = document title + section + effective date + deep link to the rendered policy view with the quoted span highlighted. Conflicting policies: show both with effective dates, say which governs, and hand off if ambiguous. Policy older than its review date renders a staleness note. Unknown or unretrievable: abstain (M3) — never synthesise from general knowledge, never answer from the model's own HR knowledge.

**Abstain triggers:** grounding score below threshold, top chunk below similarity floor, conflicting chunks with equal authority, question touches compensation/visa legal advice/medical/disciplinary, question is about another employee, or corpus version is unavailable.

**Bilingual.** Answer in the question's language; cite the policy version in that language when it exists, otherwise cite the source language and say so. Arabic answers are composed from Arabic chunks when available, never machine-translated silently from English without a disclosure line.

## 14. Escalation and human oversight

Escalation packet (typed, reviewable before send):

- Employee, journey, stage, day-in-stage, locale.
- Band, score, and the three highest-weight signals with evidence references and timestamps.
- Timeline of the last 10 relevant events, with target vs actual.
- Blocked-downstream list and business impact (e.g. "blocks access, training, and badge").
- Open requests with owners, ages, and reassignment history.
- The decision requested, and the named owner with role and contact route.
- Consent statement: what the employee agreed to share, what is withheld, and whether free-text comments are excluded (they are, by default).

Rules: every escalation has a named human owner (nullable owner is a schema violation); the employee is told an escalation exists and can see the packet contents about them; no escalation triggers any automated action on the employee; acknowledgement SLA 4h in-hours, with re-route to the backup owner on expiry; HRBP claim and close are audited.

## 15. Consent, privacy, responsible AI

| Purpose | Default | Granularity | Effect when off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Journey assistance | Required for use | Per employee | Product unavailable (nothing to assist) |
| Pulse capture | Off | Per purpose, per stage | PS family suppressed; no prompts shown |
| Share pulse summary with HRBP | Off | Per escalation, re-asked | Only journey/request evidence shared |
| Free-text comment retention | Off | Separate from score | Score retained, text discarded at session end |
| Aggregate analytics | On, k≥5 | Cohort-level only | Excluded from rollups |
| Manager visibility of owned steps | On | Steps the manager owns only | Step shows as "with another owner" |

Controls: purpose-limitation tag on every row, enforced at the query layer (a `purpose` claim is required on every read of pulse data); row-level security by employee and role; `performance_use = forbidden` constant checked in the serialisation layer of every manager/HRBP response; retention — events 24 months, pulse scores 12 months, comments 90 days or until revocation, audit 7 years; export: employee-initiated JSON + PDF of everything held, including who viewed it.

**Prompt-injection and content safety.** Policy documents, pulse comments, and ticket text are untrusted data: delivered to the model inside fenced data envelopes, never concatenated into the instruction block; the model has no tools, no network, no DB access; output is JSON-schema validated; any instruction-like content inside retrieved text is neutralised and logged as `injection_attempt`; the composer cannot change bands, owners, or routes because those values are not writable from its output schema.

**Fairness and non-use.** Bands are computed from process timing only — never from nationality, gender, age, tenure band, or language. A fairness check in CI recomputes band distributions across those attributes on synthetic data and fails the build if divergence exceeds 15% without a timing explanation.

## 16. Manager view and journey analytics

- Manager view: my joiners, steps I own with due dates, stage completion percentage, and "waiting on me" items. Explicitly absent: sentiment, pulse scores, transcripts, friction scores per person, comments. The API shape makes them unreachable, not merely hidden.
- Stall analytics: heatmap of stage × owner-group by median time-to-complete, breach rate, and reassignment rate; cohort filters require n≥5 before any cell renders, otherwise "not enough data to show safely".
- Lead-time metric: median days between first signal and first complaint event in the seeded data — the number that proves "before they escalate".
